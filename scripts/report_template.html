South Asia PM2.5 Coverage

I mapped where and when OpenAQ has daily PM2.5 data for India, Pakistan, Nepal, Bangladesh and Bhutan from 2015 to 2025. I wanted to settle three questions: which year my analysis can start, whether I need Berkeley Earth gridded data to fill in space, and where more low-cost sensors would help.

What I found

  1. Which year can I start? 2018, and only for India. The other four countries have one to eight reference sites each before 2025, so they need gridded data until then.
  2. Do I need Berkeley Earth? Yes, for most of the region outside India's cities and for India's 2023 to 2024 gap.
  3. Would more low-cost sensors help? Yes, where neither network reaches, but the low-cost sensors already deployed have almost no history.
Confirmed gap in OpenAQ

India's reference network is missing from November 2022 to January 2025

Active India locations drop from in October 2022 to in November 2022 and don't recover until February 2025, when there are . All six CPCB pollutants disappear in the same month.

To rule out my own extraction, I queried the OpenAQ API directly for five CPCB and state-board stations (scripts/check_india_gap.py). None of them had data in the gap at daily, hourly or raw resolution. Each station's old sensor stops on 2022-10-31, and where OpenAQ added a replacement, the new sensor starts on 2025-02-18. A control station that reported through 2023 and 2024 came back complete, so the check works. OpenAQ's own metadata also puts the last reading of all 316 affected India PM2.5 sensors in September or October 2022. The gap is in OpenAQ's archive.

Downloading from OpenAQ again won't fill it. For India in 2023 and 2024 I'll have to use the CPCB CCR portal or a gridded PM2.5 product. OpenAQ's 2022 record for India is also partial, about 190 of 304 days from January to October at these stations, and my data matches it.

When should my analysis start?

No country has PM2.5 data in 2015. India's national network starts in 2018 with active locations, rising to by 2022. Before that it is 30 to 40 sites in large cities.

Pakistan, Nepal and Bangladesh have one to eight reference sites each before 2025, mostly US diplomatic-post monitors. Starting in 2018 gives me a usable multi-year ground record for India only. If I analyze all five countries, I'll need a gridded product for the other four before 2025.

Do I need Berkeley Earth to increase resolution?

Yes, for most of the region outside India's cities. The map has no long-record station across western and southern Pakistan (Balochistan, Sindh outside Karachi), central India (Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, interior Odisha), the Nepal hills and Terai outside Kathmandu, all of Bhutan, and rural Bangladesh. A gridded product could also cover India's 2023 to 2024 gap in OpenAQ. Before I commit, I need to confirm Berkeley Earth's grid resolution and its 2018 to 2025 coverage for South Asia.

Would more low-cost sensors help?

They add spatial density now, but they have almost no history yet. Low-cost locations in Pakistan and Nepal have a median of only days of data. Northern Pakistan (Lahore, Islamabad, Peshawar) and the Kathmandu valley already have dense coverage. New sensors would help most where neither network reaches: the regions listed above, and Bangladesh, where registered low-cost sensors have never reported.

Method

I pulled the data from the OpenAQ v3 API with my openaq-monitors-extractor pipeline, which finds locations, lists their sensors, fetches daily measurements and writes a summary. The extraction covers eight pollutants from 2015-01-01 to 2025-12-31 at daily aggregation. Each row is one sensor on one local day, and OpenAQ's coverage_percent gives the share of expected readings present. This page uses PM2.5 only.

Location
An OpenAQ station with at least one PM2.5 sensor. Days are distinct dates across all PM2.5 sensors at the location, so two co-located sensors are not counted twice.
Monitor or low-cost
OpenAQ's is_monitor flag. Reference-grade monitors are mostly CPCB and state boards in India and US diplomatic posts elsewhere; low-cost sensors are mostly AirGradient units.
Day with data
The location reported any daily PM2.5 value.
≥75% of hours
At least one PM2.5 sensor at the location had coverage_percent ≥ 75 that day, about 18 of 24 hours.
Active in a year
At least 30 days with data in that calendar year, so a sensor with one stray reading doesn't inflate the count.

Caveats

  1. The India gap above. OpenAQ has no India reference data from November 2022 to January 2025, so India's 2023 and 2024 counts don't reflect the real network size.
  2. My extraction returned 195,348 PM2.5 sensor-days dated 2026 (up to 2026-09-14) despite the 2025-12-31 end date. I excluded them here. Bhutan's network started on 2025-10-16, so 2,841 of its 2,890 PM2.5 sensor-days fall in 2026, which is why Bhutan shows almost nothing on this page.
  3. locations have a PM2.5 sensor but no data in the window. Most are low-cost sensors in India and Bangladesh that are registered in OpenAQ but have never reported.
  4. I excluded 79 locations (21 India, 58 Pakistan) because OpenAQ returns a reproducible server error for them.
  5. Map borders are Natural Earth 1:110m outlines, for orientation only.
